Part 5 · Monetary Transformation and the Future of Real Estate · Chapter 19

A Monetary Transition Already Underway

Adoption Dynamics and Store-of-Value Preferences

The adoption dynamics surrounding bitcoin emerge gradually through voluntary adoption rather than centralized reform. In this framework, individuals adopt new monetary or savings technologies when they perceive them to offer advantages in accessibility, portability, durability, or long-term value preservation relative to existing alternatives.

Individuals who have grown up in a digitally native environment may therefore be more inclined to adopt forms of value storage that align with digital systems of communication, ownership, and exchange.

At the same time, rising barriers to entry in traditional asset classes—particularly real estate—can influence how younger cohorts approach saving and capital accumulation. When access to property ownership becomes more constrained, alternative stores of value may become relatively more attractive.

These trends remain uneven and continue to evolve across regions and economic conditions. However, they suggest that preferences for storing value are not fixed, but can shift alongside changes in technology, accessibility, cultural context, and monetary conditions—without requiring political force, regulatory compulsion, or centralized decree.

The concept of agorism was developed by Samuel Edward Konkin III, who argued that durable social and economic change emerges through voluntary exchange and the gradual adoption of alternative institutions rather than through centralized political action. Recorded lectures by Konkin discussing these ideas are available online.
